# IP-C3 Backchannel Decision

Date: 2026-07-10

## TL;DR Decision

Build C3, but reshape it from "general conversational backchannels everywhere" into a conservative **wait-gap cue layer** that composes with the shipped v4.1.0 thinking bed. Use **pre-cached, voice-matched PCM cue assets** for the core Syrinx backchannel path; generated TTS is allowed only when pre-rendered before the gap or supplied through the existing app/tool preamble hook. Do **not** add free-form optional filler strings to every tool-call schema as the primary mechanism; use app/tool metadata or `onToolCallStart` for tool-specific preambles, and keep `InteractionDecision.backchannel` for policy-timed nonsemantic cues. Rule-based mode should emit **no user-pause/mid-utterance backchannels**; it may emit at most one generic cue on the G3 delayed tool-call phase, gated by audio state and `caps.emitsBackchannel`. VAP mode, when built, owns real listener-backchannel timing with cooldowns and the same render/gating path.

## Decision Frame

IP-C3 is not deciding whether Syrinx should make agents sound more casual in general. Syrinx already has three adjacent mechanisms:

- Endpoint latency filler: `LatencyFillerController` speaks short connectives at end-of-turn before LLM TTFT and marks `turn_latency.fillerUsed` so filler is not counted as relevant first answer (`packages/core/src/latency-filler.ts`, `packages/core/src/voice-agent-session.ts`).
- Tool-call thinking lifecycle: G3 emits `tool_call_cue` phases `started`, `delayed`, `complete`, `failed`, with `delayCueAfterMs` defaulting to 2000 ms (`packages/core/src/voice-agent-session.ts`).
- Background thinking bed: `wireBackgroundThinking()` turns the mixer thinking loop on for `started`/`delayed` and off for `complete`/`failed` (`packages/server-websocket/src/background-audio.ts`).

The decision is whether a discrete verbal cue has a job that the thinking bed does not. The evidence says yes, but only narrowly: a bed says "the line is alive"; a short cue says "I am still with you" and is useful when the wait crosses a human-noticeable threshold. The evidence also says poorly timed or over-frequent backchannels are worse than none, so rule-based mode must stay conservative.

## What The Prior Art Actually Says

There are three different features often called "filler" or "backchannel":

1. **User-backchannel detection**: decide that the user's "uh-huh" during assistant speech is not an interruption. Pipecat's Krisp VIVA strategy and Syrinx's existing arbiter tests both sit here (`pipecat-ai/pipecat:src/pipecat/turns/user_start/krisp_viva_ip_user_turn_start_strategy.py`, `packages/core/src/turn-arbiter.ts`).
2. **Assistant latency masking**: speak "let me check" or play thinking audio while a tool/LLM runs. LiveKit `with_filler`, Deepgram `agent_filler`, Vapi flush, OpenAI preambles, and Syrinx's thinking bed sit here.
3. **Assistant listener backchannels**: "mhmm", "yeah", "got it" while the user still has the floor. GPT-Live and VAP-backchannel research sit here; this is the timing-sensitive one.

Syrinx C3 should not merge these into one mechanism. The shipped thinking bed already solves baseline wait comfort, especially on telephony where silence reads as a dead call (`packages/server-websocket/src/background-audio.ts`). A verbal cue adds value only when the system wants to convey attention or progress, and it must not be confused with the final answer or with turn-taking.

The academic evidence supports a learned timing model for true listener backchannels. VAP was introduced as a self-supervised, frame-incremental turn-taking objective and includes tasks for upcoming turn-shifts and backchannels (arXiv:2205.09812). The VAP backchannel fine-tuning paper focuses on short, frequent listener tokens and predicts both timing and type continuously on real dialogue (arXiv:2410.15929; NAACL 2025 PDF). TurnGPT supports the text/semantic side because pragmatic completeness helps turn-shift prediction, but it is not sufficient for audio-timed backchannels alone (arXiv:2010.10874). Social/HRI work adds the product constraint: backchannels help when they signal attention without taking the floor, but too frequent, intrusive, or culture-mismatched cues reduce satisfaction (Frontiers Robotics and AI 2023; EMNLP Findings 2024 engagement paper).

## Asset vs Schema-Filler Analysis

### Option A: Pre-cached PCM cues

Use a small per-voice asset set such as `mm_hmm`, `yeah`, `got_it`, possibly with 2-3 takes per voice. Render them through `BackgroundAudioMixer`, duck/fade them like the existing bed, and keep them out of the main TTS transcript.

Pros:

- Lowest hot-path latency: no TTS call on the delayed cue path. This matches Syrinx's REQ-9 posture and avoids adding a network dependency during the exact gap being masked (`docs/rfc-interaction-policy-seam.md` §12-Q2).
- Clean composition with the shipped mixer. `BackgroundAudioMixer` already caches/resamples PCM sources, mixes under speech, emits idle frames, and is runtime-neutral for workerd (`packages/server-websocket/src/background-audio.ts`).
- Does not pollute LLM/TTS context. Pipecat's example explicitly avoids appending a filler to context to preserve optimization behavior (`pipecat-ai/pipecat:examples/function-calling/function-calling-openai-responses-async.py`).
- Safer semantics: a nonsemantic "mm-hmm" or "still with you" cue does not promise tool success or specific facts.

Cons:

- Needs voice-matched assets. A cue in a different voice than the assistant is jarring; research on backchannel synthesis warns that natural examples need enough inventory and prosody control.
- Less expressive and less tool-specific than generated speech.
- Locale/culture mismatch is real. The default asset set should be conservative and configurable.

Verdict: **Use this for core C3.** It is the only mechanism that satisfies Syrinx's no-hot-path-RPC posture and fits the existing mixer.

### Option B: Generated on-demand cues

Call the active TTS/realtime model to synthesize "mhmm" or "let me check" at the moment the gap is detected.

Pros:

- Voice/style can match the current TTS voice automatically.
- Can be language-specific and tool-specific.
- Matches LiveKit's `with_filler`, Deepgram's injected filler, Pipecat's `TTSSpeakFrame`, and OpenAI Realtime preamble patterns.

Cons:

- Adds the exact synthesis round trip C3 is supposed to hide. For network TTS, this can turn "no cue" into "late cue plus final answer collision."
- Harder to cancel cleanly, especially if the real answer arrives while cue synthesis is still pending.
- Risks transcript/context pollution unless the system explicitly marks the cue as non-answer audio.

How to avoid hot-path latency if generated:

- Pre-render approved cue strings at session start or voice selection time and store PCM in the mixer cache.
- Pre-render at `tool_call_cue.started`, but only play at `delayed` if the tool is still pending. If synthesis is not ready by the delayed deadline, skip rather than play late.
- Allow app-owned generated preambles through `onToolCallStart` when the app accepts that latency/quality tradeoff. Do not make core C3 depend on this.

Verdict: **Not the core path.** Generated cues are acceptable as a higher-level preamble feature only if pre-rendered before playback is needed.

### Option C: Tool-call optional filler fields

Add fields to tool/delegate schema such as `filler`, `preamble`, or `speakWhileWorking`, and let Syrinx voice that during the gap.

Pros:

- Tool authors know what status message is semantically honest. "Checking your order status" is better than generic "one sec" when the tool is actually an order lookup.
- Deepgram's public example is a strong schema-driven reference: `agent_filler` is a function with a constrained enum and prompts forbid direct filler phrases (`deepgram/voice-agent-function-calling:functions.py`, `client.py`).
- Retell and LiveKit both encourage speaking during long actions, but through controlled node/tool APIs rather than random background audio.

Cons:

- Free-form filler fields pollute the tool schema with presentation concerns and create a second speech-control plane alongside G3 `tool_call_cue`.
- Model-supplied text can overclaim: "I found it" before the tool result exists, or "booking now" before confirmation.
- It does not solve true backchannel timing. It is a tool preamble mechanism, not a listener-backchannel policy.
- It can duplicate `onToolCallStart`, `tool_call_cue`, and the thinking bed unless constrained to those existing lifecycle hooks.

Verdict: **Do not add generic free-form filler fields to tool calls.** If tool-specific speech is needed, add a constrained cue reference in tool/app metadata, for example `latencyCue: { started?: CueRef, delayed?: CueRef }`, where `CueRef` points to an approved asset or pre-rendered phrase. Keep the actual trigger on G3 phases and keep rendering in the existing mixer/TTS path. For apps that need full control, `withVoice({ onToolCallStart })` already provides an isolated app hook (`packages/cf-agents/src/with-voice.ts`).

### Option D: Hybrid

Use asset cues for generic nonsemantic backchannels, and tool/app metadata or `onToolCallStart` for explicit preambles.

Verdict: **Pick hybrid.** It matches prior art:

- LiveKit separates thinking/background audio from tool-scoped fillers (`BackgroundAudioPlayer`, `with_filler`).
- Deepgram uses a schema-driven filler tool for lookup preambles, not generic "backchannel everywhere."
- OpenAI GPT-Live handles listener backchannels model-natively, while Realtime docs treat preambles as short spoken updates before tool/reasoning work.
- MoshiRAG keeps real-time interaction in the full-duplex front while retrieval runs asynchronously, with lightweight pre-RAG content rather than waiting in silence.

## Recommendation For Syrinx

### 1. Build C3, but define two cue classes

Add a narrow cue taxonomy rather than a generic string free-for-all:

- `backchannel`: short nonsemantic listener cue, for example `mm_hmm` or `yeah`.
- `status_preamble`: semantically honest tool progress cue, for example `checking_that`.

The already-built `InteractionDecision` can keep `{ kind: "backchannel", cue }`, but the `cue` should be a stable cue id, not arbitrary user/model text. Rendering maps cue id plus voice id to a PCM asset or pre-rendered PCM cache entry.

### 2. Compose with the thinking bed

Do not replace the v4.1.0 thinking bed. Keep it as the low-risk continuous wait affordance:

- `tool_call_cue.started` turns the thinking loop on, as it does today.
- `tool_call_cue.complete` and `failed` turn it off, as they do today.
- A discrete cue is optional and sparse, mixed over the bed with short ducking/fade.

The bed is the default "alive" signal; the verbal cue is a threshold-crossing "still with you" signal. This avoids double audio at gap open.

### 3. Rule-based trigger

Rule-based mode should **not** attempt listener backchannels on arbitrary user pauses or mid-utterance silence. The sources are consistent that timing needs audio/prosody/semantic prediction, and VAP/TurnGPT exist because fixed silence thresholds interrupt or lag.

For the rule-based policy shipping now:

- Trigger candidate: the G3 `tool_call_cue.delayed` phase only, not `started`.
- Default timing: use current `delayCueAfterMs` default, 2000 ms, as the first eligible moment.
- Emit at most one cue per tool call / delegate gap.
- Suppress if assistant TTS is already active, the final answer has started, the call is completing, the user is speaking, no cue asset exists for the active voice/locale, or `caps.emitsBackchannel` is true.
- Do not repeat by default. If a future product config allows repeats, use a long interval, for example 8-10 seconds, and a hard max of 2, because HRI evidence warns over-frequency is harmful.

Implementation shape:

- Prefer feeding `delegate_state`/G3 phase into the policy and returning `InteractionDecision.backchannel` so metrics and `caps.emitsBackchannel` stay in the InteractionPolicy path.
- If current `InteractionObservation` is too coarse (`delegateInFlight?: boolean` only), extend it with a small lifecycle field rather than inventing a second event bus path.

### 4. VAP trigger

When `VapInteractionPolicy` lands:

- Use `p_backchannel > TH` only while the user has the floor or while a delegate gap is open and the system is not speaking.
- Keep a cooldown, one-cue-per-gap cap, and locale/voice asset availability check.
- Keep `caps.emitsBackchannel` as a hard suppressor.
- For true listener backchannels, prefer `mm_hmm`/`yeah` cues over status phrases. Status phrases belong to G3/tool metadata.

### 5. Capability gating

`caps.emitsBackchannel` means Syrinx must not render its own verbal backchannel cue. It should still allow the thinking bed unless the front/provider also owns the entire wait affordance and the app disables `backgroundAudio.thinking`. `caps.supportsFullDuplex` already makes Syrinx observe-only for interaction decisions; in that mode C3 should only render app-explicit G3 status cues if the app asked for them, not policy backchannels.

## Implementation Sketch

This is a build sketch, not production code.

### Core packets and policy

- `packages/core/src/interaction-policy.ts`
  - Treat `InteractionDecision.backchannel.cue` as a cue id.
  - Extend `InteractionObservation` for delegate state with `toolCallPhase?: "started" | "delayed" | "complete" | "failed"` or a similarly narrow lifecycle field.
- `packages/core/src/interaction-coordinator.ts`
  - Subscribe to the existing G3 tool/delegate lifecycle source.
  - On decision `{ kind: "backchannel", cue }`, emit `interaction.backchannel` only if `!caps.emitsBackchannel`.
  - Add metrics: `backchannel.candidate`, `backchannel.emitted`, `backchannel.suppressed_caps`, `backchannel.suppressed_tts_active`, `backchannel.suppressed_missing_asset`, `backchannel.suppressed_user_speaking`.
- `packages/core/src/packets.ts`
  - Add or finalize `InteractionBackchannelPacket`: `{ contextId, timestampMs, cue }`.

### Rule policy

- `packages/core/src/policies/rule-based.ts`
  - Keep interruption behavior unchanged.
  - Add a tiny delegate-gap state machine:
    - on `toolCallPhase: "started"`: mark gap open and cue not played;
    - on `"delayed"`: if gap open and cue not played, return one backchannel cue id;
    - on `"complete" | "failed"` or reset: clear.
  - Do not inspect user silence for backchannel emission.

### Mixer and server render

- `packages/server-websocket/src/background-audio.ts`
  - Add one-shot cue source support, separate from looped ambient/thinking sources.
  - Mix one-shot cues through the same PCM/resample/clip path.
  - Duck/fade the thinking bed briefly under the cue so "mm-hmm" is intelligible.
- Server outbound paths (`edge.ts`, `edge-twilio.ts`, `telnyx.ts`, `smartpbx.ts` or their current equivalents)
  - Listen for `interaction.backchannel`.
  - Resolve cue id to active voice/locale PCM asset.
  - Queue the one-shot cue into the existing mixer.
- `packages/cf-agents/src/with-voice.ts`
  - Pass optional backchannel cue assets through `backgroundAudio`, not a new top-level mechanism.
  - Leave `onToolCallStart` as the app-owned preamble hook.

### Assets

Minimum asset set:

- Per supported voice: `mm_hmm`, `yeah`, `got_it` as mono PCM16 at 16 kHz or 24 kHz.
- Duration target: 250-900 ms.
- Gain-normalized and trimmed, with no leading/trailing dead air beyond intentional onset.
- Locale tags. Do not use English cues for non-English voices unless explicitly configured.

Generated path:

- Optional build/session step that pre-renders configured cue strings to PCM before any delayed gap.
- No runtime network TTS call may block the delayed cue trigger.

### Tests

- Core policy:
  - rule-based emits no backchannel on VAD/user pause observations;
  - rule-based emits exactly one cue on `started -> delayed`;
  - complete/failed clears state;
  - reset clears state;
  - `caps.emitsBackchannel` suppresses packet emission.
- Mixer:
  - one-shot cue is audible in an idle frame;
  - thinking loop continues before/after cue;
  - cue is not emitted when no source exists;
  - clipping stays bounded.
- Session/server:
  - simulated `tool_call_cue.delayed` produces `interaction.backchannel`;
  - TTS active suppresses or schedules safely according to final chosen render rule;
  - realtime full-duplex caps suppress policy backchannel.

### Listen smoke

Add a smoke similar to `smoke:background-audio-listen`:

1. Render a 6-second delegate gap with thinking bed only.
2. Render the same gap with one cue at 2 seconds.
3. Render with cue while a final answer starts near the delayed threshold to verify no collision.
4. Write WAVs and a JSON event trace: cue candidates, suppression reasons, first-answer time.

Pass condition: cue is intelligible, not clipped, not repeated, not late, and does not mask the first answer.

## Open Risks And Flip Conditions

- **Asset quality risk:** poor or mismatched cues are worse than silence. Flip to generated/pre-rendered cues if voice vendors provide reliable low-latency cached micro-utterances per voice.
- **Cultural/localization risk:** "mm-hmm" and "yeah" do not transfer cleanly across languages and domains. Disable cue assets by default for unsupported locales.
- **Overuse risk:** engagement studies support backchannels, but HRI work warns over-frequency/intrusiveness reduces satisfaction. Keep rule-based to one delayed cue and require listen/eval before repeats.
- **Metric distortion risk:** filler/backchannel audio must not count as the first relevant answer. Extend `turn_latency`/metrics so C3 cue use is visible, following Syrinx's existing `fillerUsed` precedent.
- **VAP quality risk:** if VAP p99 or live timing does not meet the latency/listen gate, keep VAP backchannels disabled and ship only G3 delayed cue support.
- **Provider-native front risk:** if GPT-Live/Gemini/other fronts emit high-quality native backchannels, `caps.emitsBackchannel` should suppress Syrinx cues and the thinking bed should become an app choice.

## Final Call

Build IP-C3 as a small, conservative cue renderer over the existing G3 thinking lifecycle and `BackgroundAudioMixer`. Do not build a new filler subsystem. Do not turn rule-based policy into a fake VAP by triggering on user pauses. Do add the asset-backed cue path now because it composes cleanly with the shipped thinking bed, gives apps a deterministic latency affordance, and leaves the richer listener-backchannel problem for VAP behind the same `InteractionDecision.backchannel` and `caps.emitsBackchannel` gate.
